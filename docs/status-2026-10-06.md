## Run 17 findings (2026-10-04)
- **Layer off-by-one:** C8 plane l sits at depth (l + 1) × 500 m along the axis (`c8_air_shower.cpp`; checked on the
  C8 showers). `place_clouds_enu` used l × 500 for Up, so heights were off by 500 · d_Up. Fixed on run18-showerframe.
- **Kernel frame:** the kernel matched in global North–Up with 500 m East slabs, which is right only for horizontal
  West-going showers. Malata, with taus heading North into a South-facing slope, was the worst case. Replaced by
  the shower-frame kernel.
- **Quantized arrival times:** the leading edge was a plane's time, so times jumped in 500 m / c ≈ 1.6 μs steps. Fixed
  with t = t_p + (s_det − s_p) / c, i.e. interpolated between planes. The recon's direction leans mostly on T.
- **C8 sideways shift:** C8 showers within ~30° of the simulation's field (B along root-x = our East/West) come out as
  a narrow band shifted sideways by 0.2–0.4 × depth (12–21°) toward −y. Present in the training showers and in
  Hamza's generated ones; not in our code. Suspected C8 tracking for particles moving along B; unconfirmed. The raw
  parquet output is deleted. Open questions for Hamza: the B units (−2.5 nT, −4.3 nT, probably meant μT) and whether
  x means North.
- **Generator training range:** zenith 60–100°, all azimuths, log10 E 5–7.7. Run 17 had 35% of showers above 100°.

- **Grid, 100 detectors:**
  - good at 5° with |Δlog10 E| < 0.2: 38%

  - within 2.5°: 45%

- **Kernel-in-the-loop optimizations:** from the grid start they end within ±1.5 points of the grid (best within 1°:
  13.2% vs 12.2%). From the center start they spread but end lower (14–75% within 5°).
- **Detector count** (grid, within 5°): 4 → 8%, 10 → 23%, 20 → 36%, 25 → 38%, 50 → 65%, 100 → 77%.
  Within 1°: 0.5 / 1.6 / 2.5 / 3.8 / 7.3 / 12.2%.
- **Disks of N detectors at the plane centre:** best at 3 km radius (≈ covering the plane): 79 / 70 / 46% within 5° for
  N = 100 / 50 / 20. They beat the grid at 20 and 50 detectors, because the square grid leaves gaps there.
- **Compact cores (100–500 m):** 5–21% within 5° and < 1.2% within 1° at every N.
- **Hybrids:** they improve with how much is spread out, but stay below the grid.
- **Clustered grids** (K clusters × M detectors, 50–500 m): lose at 1°, 2.5° and 5° for N = 20, 50 and 100. Small
  clusters are worst; the best are the most spread out.
- **Footprint on the grid:** median radius holding 50% of the signal ≈ 300 m and 90% ≈ 440 m. Grid spacing ≈ 500 m;
  nearest detector ≈ 210 m from the axis; median 4 detectors with ≥ 1 count.
- **Signal below 1 count:** showers with no detector at ≥ 1 count still get 3.3°, so the recon uses those signals.
- **N < 100:** empty recon slots set to 0 vs detectors repeated changes scores by 1–4 points.
- **Diagnostics** (under `<run18>/diagnostics/`): `core_test`, `angleonly`, `detector_count`, `footprint`,
  `core_test_ndet` (`score_pad.py`), `clusters`.

## Why nothing beats the grid
- **Same average for every layout:** showers land uniformly on the plane, so with N detectors over area A, every layout
  gives the same mean number of detectors within r of a shower (N π r² / A).
- **Clustering only shares them out:** many detectors for the few showers landing on a cluster, none for the rest.
- **Tight clusters give short baselines:** the direction comes from timing, which needs long baselines.
- **Possible bias:** the recon was trained on spread-out 100-detector layouts, so it may favour them.

## Open, decisions pending
1. **Layout-agnostic recon** (the user's last question). The recon is DeepSets with maxmean pooling, so it is already
   invariant to detector order. The options:
   - **Variable N:** train on random detector counts, with a mask channel and masked pooling instead of zero or
     repeated slots.
   - **More layouts:** add clusters, cores, rings (`layout_rings` exists in `modules/layouts/strategies.py`), scaled
     grids and disks to the Stage 1 strategies. Order-sensitive: append only.
   - **On-the-fly labels:** generate kernel labels per batch for random layouts, so every batch is a new layout.
   - **Translation invariance:** detector positions relative to the signal centroid.
   - **For contrast:** SWGOLO reconstructs with a likelihood fit, which works for any layout.

   Then re-run the core, cluster and detector-count tests.
